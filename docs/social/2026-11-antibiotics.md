text:
Antimicrobial resistance is usually told as a story about careless patients: the uncle who feels better on day three and stops his course. I think it is a story about access.

In much of India an antibiotic is cheaper than the auto fare to the clinic and takes two minutes at the chemist. A doctor and a test can cost a day's wages and a day's wait. Meanwhile, a 2024 Lancet study estimates that drug-resistant infections directly killed about 1.1 million people worldwide in 2021, and forecasts 39 million deaths between 2025 and 2050, with South Asia hit hardest. New antibiotics are slow to come, because a drug doctors rightly keep in reserve is a terrible business.

The fix is not one more poster. It is making the right thing as easy as the wrong one: a quick, cheap test that says "viral, go home", a chemist who can say no without losing the customer, and farms that cannot buy hospital drugs by the sack.
